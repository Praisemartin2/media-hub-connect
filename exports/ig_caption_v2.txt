🚨 China just lost 20 YEARS of home price growth.

Adjusted for inflation, Chinese home prices have fallen below their 2006 level — down roughly 25% from the 2021 peak (BIS real price index). In early 2026, only 4 of 70 major cities saw new-home prices rise, and not one saw resale prices climb (China National Bureau of Statistics). Beijing even scrapped the lending rules that triggered the crash — and prices are STILL falling, with a Reuters poll projecting ~4% more downside this year.

Here's what it teaches investors 👇

1️⃣ APPRECIATION IS NOT A STRATEGY
Chinese buyers assumed prices only go up. They've now fallen five straight years (NBS). If your deal only works when prices rise, it's not a deal — it's a bet. Buy for monthly cash flow.

2️⃣ CONCENTRATION TURNS CORRECTIONS INTO CATASTROPHES
Housing made up nearly 70% of Chinese urban household wealth (People's Bank of China survey, cited by the IMF). When prices fell, family net worth fell with them. Diversify how you hold wealth.

3️⃣ SUPPLY IS THE WHOLE GAME
China overbuilt — tens of millions of units sit vacant or unfinished (NBER, Rogoff & Yang). The U.S. has the opposite problem: a shortage of 3.7–4.7 MILLION homes (Freddie Mac; Zillow). Same asset. Opposite fundamentals.

4️⃣ LEVERAGE + POLICY CAN TURN OVERNIGHT
Evergrande was carrying $300B+ in debt when the "Three Red Lines" rules changed — it became the world's most indebted developer and collapsed (Reuters; Bloomberg). Borrow so you survive the rules changing.

5️⃣ CORRECTIONS TAKE YEARS, NOT QUARTERS
Economists compare China's unwind to Japan's "lost decade" (IMF). Underwrite deals that survive a long, flat market — not ones that need a quick bounce.

Bottom line: real estate still builds wealth — when you buy cash flow in undersupplied markets. That's the game we play. 🇺🇸🔑
